The Impact of Earthworms on the Global Economy

A rigorous, peer-unreviewed treatment of the Wormi–Macroeconomic Coupling Hypothesis.

News23 Nov 2026 · 11 min read

Abstract

We present the Wormi–Macroeconomic Coupling Hypothesis (WMCH): that the earthworm, and specifically the Native Inuit Gregetian Wormi of Nepal, is the sole exogenous driver of lunar phase, Ethiopian tidal pool height, school timetabling and the global economy. From a sample of n = 2 (one researcher, and his cousin) we recover a coupling coefficient that is significant at p < 0.05, provided the reader does not ask follow-up questions. Known limitations include the absence of an ocean. All data, equations and citations are fictional; Section 8 explains what is actually true.

1. Introduction

When one thinks of the world economy they must first consider the reach of the Native Inuit Gregetian Earthworm society of Nepal. Although the Chinese may deny it, the earthworm (or Wormi as named in Ancient Indian Proverbial Text) are vital for many aspects of life such as the moon phases, Ethiopian Tidal Pools, School timetables and the Global Economy, all of which are, according to leading economists who have never been named, wriggled into existence by a single committee of Wormi that meets every Thursday at 3:47 a.m. beneath a Himalayan car park.

This entry formalises that claim. Each of the four dependent systems is modelled below as a coupling to a single latent variable, the collective Wormi activity state, and each model is accompanied by a figure, a table of variables or, in one regrettable case, a cross-section of a compost heap. A symbol glossary appears in Table 3.

2. The lunar tether

The moon does not orbit the Earth of its own accord. Each night roughly Nw ≈ 4 × 109 Wormi burrow in a coordinated clockwise direction and tug the moon along by means of a theoretical string (Figure 1). The tension carried by the string depends on the lunar phase angle φ:

T(φ) = ½ · Nw · μw · vb2 · (1 − cos φ) Eq. 1 · Lunar tether tension (reported in joules, a convention the Society refuses to revisit)

With a mean mass μw of 4 g and a burrowing velocity vb of 1.2 cm/min, peak tension at the full moon is about 0.64 J, which the Society describes as “plenty”. At the new moon (φ = 0) the term in brackets vanishes and the string goes slack. This is Dark Biscuit Hour, a collective tea break during which the moon is held in place only by habit.

3. Ethiopian tidal pools

Ethiopia has been landlocked since 1993, which is precisely why its tidal pools are so impressive. We model pool height as a sinusoid driven by the Wormi digestive cycle, lagged by a phase offset δ:

h(t) = h0 + A · sin(ωdt − δ),   ωd = 2π / Td Eq. 2 · Pool height under digestive forcing

With a digestive period Td of 24 h and δ = π/3, the pools peak four hours after a large Wormi lunch (Figure 2). The sample comprised one researcher and his cousin (n = 2); the lag was significant at p < 0.05 for the researcher and “sort of” for the cousin. We call the resulting phenomenon an endorheic tide: a tide that has no ocean to go out to and goes out regardless.

4. School timetables

Few people realise that the 45-minute lesson was invented by an earthworm. Specifically, a Wormi named Gerald, who lacked a watch but had strong opinions about double maths. Lesson length is the time taken for one Wormi to cross a standard footpath of length Lf at crawl speed vw:

τlesson = Lf / vw = 180 cm / (4 cm/min) = 45 min Eq. 3 · Gerald’s Law of Lesson Duration

Recess follows from surfacing behaviour after rain. If R is rainfall in millimetres and λ = 0.31 mm−1 is the Society’s emergence constant, the probability that a given Wormi is on the footpath is:

P(surface) = 1 − e−λR Eq. 4 · Post-rain surfacing probability

5. The global economy

We arrive at the heart of the matter. World currencies are not backed by gold but by a Central Worm Reserve stored in a compost heap beneath an undisclosed location in Nepal (Figure 5). Each currency is pegged to the weighted wriggle-rate of its national Wormi population, known on trading floors as the Squirm Index:

S(t) = (1/N) Σi wi(t) · ρi,   Eab = Sa / Sb Eq. 5 · Squirm Index and the exchange rate it implies

Inflation, long a mystery, is what happens when too many Wormi are born in the same quarter and demand a raise. Recessions occur during dry spells, when the Wormi retreat deeper underground and refuse to answer their phones:

πt = α · (Bt − Bt−1) / Bt−1,   Rt = 1 if Σj dj > D* Eq. 6 · Wage-demand inflation and the dry-spell recession indicator

Figure 3 traces the transmission mechanism. Figure 4 plots a simulated Squirm Index with two dry-spell recessions marked. When the index falls, markets panic and analysts shout “Soil is sinking!” on live television.

6. Methods and limitations

Wriggle rates were measured with a ruler held by Gerald. The Wormi population estimate (Nw) was obtained by asking the Wormi, who declined to answer on the grounds that the question was “a bit much for a Thursday”. The following limitations should be borne in mind:

  • Sample size. One researcher and his cousin. The cousin has since asked to be removed from the study.
  • Unobserved confounders. The Chinese denial remains unmodelled, as do the economists who have never been named.
  • Instrumentation. The tether has never been observed directly. Its existence is inferred from the fact that the moon has not left.
  • Geography. Ethiopia has no ocean. This has not stopped the pools.
  • Replication. Attempts were disrupted by weather, which is also Wormi-driven, so the confound is circular.

7. Policy recommendations

  1. Central banks should publish the Squirm Index alongside, and with equal prominence to, the interest rate.
  2. An independent audit of the Central Worm Reserve, conducted by someone who is not afraid of compost.
  3. A statutory minimum of one Dark Biscuit Hour per lunar cycle, to be protected from industrial action.
  4. A seat on the board of the Reserve Bank for Gerald, who has earned it.
When the Squirm Index sneezes, the world catches a cold. When it takes a tea break, so does the moon.

8. Field notes: what is actually true

Reality check

Everything above is made up, including the Gregetian society, the committee, the Squirm Index and Gerald. Earthworms do not control the moon, tides or the economy. Here is what the real animals do:

  • Charles Darwin spent decades studying earthworms and published his last book on them in 1881, The Formation of Vegetable Mould through the Action of Worms. It remains a classic of soil science.
  • Earthworms breathe through their skin, are hermaphrodites, and have several pairs of aortic arches that act as hearts.
  • Their burrowing and feeding mix and aerate soil, and shape soil structure and nutrient cycling. That is a real contribution to agriculture, and so to economies, just not a magical one.
  • School lessons are the product of timetabling committees, not Gerald.
